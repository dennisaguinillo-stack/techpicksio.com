Content Creator Gear Checklist (Free Printable)

Last updated: August 27, 2026 · Research-based guide · 4 min read

Take four measurements, then buy four things. Most gear a new creator returns was bought without checking whether it physically fits their phone or plugs into it. This checklist front-loads those four checks, lists the four essentials in the order worth buying them, and names what to leave alone for now. Measure these four things first

Every one of these decides whether something you buy will work with the phone you own. They take about two minutes and they prevent the most common reason gear goes back.

  • Phone width and length in millimetres, with your case on — cage clamp ranges are stated in mm, and a phone that fits bare often does not fit cased.
  • Your phone’s connector (USB-C or Lightning) — a microphone with the wrong one is unusable until an adapter arrives.
  • Whether your phone has a MagSafe magnet ring — iPhones do, Android phones do not, and that single fact rules half the cage market in or out.
  • How long a typical shoot runs for you — this is the number that decides whether a microphone’s battery life and a light’s runtime are adequate.

The four essentials

In buying order. Each is more useful once the one above it is in place, so working down the list never leaves anything sitting in a drawer.

  • A phone that shoots 4K — almost certainly the one in your pocket. Do not upgrade it to improve video; the money goes further everywhere else on this list.
  • A wireless lavalier microphone — the largest single improvement available. See wireless lavalier mics under $100.
  • A cage or grip that fits your measured phone width — steadies framing and gives the mic and light somewhere to mount. See phone video cages, MagSafe cages or Android cages.
  • One pocket LED light, chosen on CRI rather than brightness. See budget LED video lights.

Worth adding next

None of these are needed on day one. Each becomes obvious the moment a specific shot needs it.

  • A tripod or stand — move this up to second if you film yourself alone.
  • An ND filter, if you shoot outdoors — it is what holds a cinematic shutter angle in daylight. See ND filters and phone lenses.
  • Fast external storage, once you record in high-bitrate formats — sustained write speed is the only spec that matters. See portable SSDs for ProRes.
  • A spare cable and a power bank — unglamorous, and the two things most likely to end a shoot early.

Leave these for later

Not bad purchases — just badly timed ones. Each has a condition that makes it worth revisiting.

  • A gimbal — revisit when handheld movement is genuinely limiting a shot. See the DJI Osmo Mobile 6 review.
  • A drone — revisit when aerial shots are part of your format, and check take-off weight against local rules first. See the sub-250g drone comparison.
  • Anamorphic or add-on lenses — revisit once your footage is already well lit and well framed.
  • A second camera body of any kind — revisit when a shot you need is impossible, not when footage feels unpolished. See phone-only content creation.

Before your first shoot

Five checks that catch the problems which are cheap to fix beforehand and expensive to discover in the edit.

  • Phone clamped in the cage first, tightened evenly from both sides so it does not sit crooked.
  • Microphone on the top cold shoe, not the side — a side mount can end up aimed at your own hand.
  • LED light set to 30–40% output to start. Phone sensors handle highlights poorly and it is almost always enough.
  • Cables routed through the cage’s channels before you shoot, not after.
  • A 10-second test clip, walking normally, played back with headphones. This catches loose screws, cable rattle and clipped audio while they are still easy to fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should be on a content creator gear checklist?

Four essentials and four measurements. The essentials are a phone that shoots 4K, a cage or grip, a wireless lavalier microphone and one pocket LED light. The measurements are your phone's width and length with its case on, the connector your phone uses, whether your phone has a MagSafe magnet ring, and how long a typical shoot runs — those four decide whether the gear you buy actually fits and lasts.

Can I download this checklist?

Yes. There is a plain-text version linked from this page that you can save to a phone or print, and the page itself is formatted to print cleanly from any browser with Ctrl+P or Cmd+P — the navigation, disclosure banner and links drop away and only the checklist prints.

What measurements should I take before buying a phone cage?

Measure your phone's width and length with the case you actually use fitted, in millimetres. Cage listings state a clamp range, and the most common return reason is a phone that fits bare but not in its case. If you use a thick rugged case, measure it rather than looking up the phone's bare dimensions.

Do I need everything on the checklist before I start filming?

No. The checklist is ordered, and you can publish with nothing on it beyond a phone. Work down it as budget allows — the microphone alone changes more than the rest of the list combined, and each later item is more useful once the earlier ones are in place.
