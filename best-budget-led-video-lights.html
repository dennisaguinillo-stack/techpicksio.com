Buyer's Guide: What to Look For

Three specs matter most for a budget mobile light. CRI (Color Rendering Index) should be 95 or higher — below that, skin tones and colors look inaccurate on camera and are hard to fix in editing. Battery capacity determines how long you can shoot; 2000mAh is fine for short clips, but 4000-5000mAh is worth it for longer sessions. Mounting is easy to overlook: a magnetic back and multiple cold-shoe points make a light far more versatile on a phone rig. A wide color-temperature range (2500-9000K) is a bonus that lets you match ambient light indoors and out.

Frequently Asked Questions

What CRI should I look for in a video light?

Aim for CRI 95 or higher. Below that, skin tones and colors render inaccurately on camera and are difficult to correct in editing. Every light in this guide is rated 95 or above.

Are cheap LED panels good enough for video?

For fill light and accent lighting, yes. Budget pocket panels handle brightening a face or adding colored background light well. What they can't do is act as a key light for a large scene, since output is limited.

How much battery life do I need in a pocket LED?

2000mAh is workable for short clips, but 4000-5000mAh is worth it for longer sessions. Note that running at full brightness or using RGB modes drains the battery considerably faster than the rated figure suggests.

What does RGB mean on a video light?

RGB lights can produce full-color output, not just white at different temperatures. That's useful for colored background accents and effects. If you only need clean white light on a subject, a bi-color light without RGB is usually cheaper.
