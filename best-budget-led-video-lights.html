Buyer's Guide: What Actually Decides This Purchase

Three specs matter, and they are not equally important. CRI is the one to buy on. Anything under 95 renders skin tones inaccurately on camera and fights you in the edit — no amount of battery or mounting flexibility rescues a light that makes your subject look unwell. Mounting comes second, and it's the spec people most often regret ignoring: a magnetic back plus multiple cold-shoe points is the difference between repositioning a light in five seconds and dismantling your rig to do it. Battery is third — 2000mAh genuinely is enough for short clips, and only matters if you shoot long sessions away from power.

So: buy the Neewer RGB61. It's the only light here that wins on the first two specs at once, and battery is the cheapest of the three problems to solve — you can carry a power bank, but you cannot fix bad color rendering after the fact. Buy something else on this list only for the specific reasons we name above: a wider beam, a bigger battery, or a cheaper accent light.

Frequently Asked Questions

What CRI should I look for in a video light?

Aim for CRI 95 or higher. Below that, skin tones and colors render inaccurately on camera and are difficult to correct in editing. Every light in this guide is rated 95 or above.

Are cheap LED panels good enough for video?

For fill and accent lighting, yes — and that's what most mobile creators actually need. A budget pocket panel will brighten a face or add colored background light perfectly well. What it will not do is key-light a large scene; if that's your job, none of the lights on this page is the right purchase and you should be shopping a different category entirely.

How much battery life do I need in a pocket LED?

Less than you'd think. 2000mAh covers short clips fine, so don't pay a premium for capacity unless you actually shoot long sessions away from power — in which case buy the 4000mAh Neewer SL90C. Either way, budget for reality: full brightness and RGB modes drain these panels considerably faster than the rated figure implies.

What does RGB mean on a video light?

RGB lights can produce full-color output, not just white at different temperatures. That's useful for colored background accents and effects. If you only need clean white light on a subject, a bi-color light without RGB is usually cheaper.
