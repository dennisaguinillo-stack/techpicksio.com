Monitor arm — editor’s choice

A gas-spring arm frees the desk surface a camera or capture card otherwise competes for, and lets the display tilt out of frame for on-camera segments. The specification worth checking before anything else is the VESA pattern and weight range against your specific monitor — an arm rated below your display’s weight will sag over months, not immediately, which is why it is easy to miss in a quick test.

USB-C dock — budget pick

A single-cable dock is worth it the moment a desk has more than one capture source. The number that matters is the display-output bandwith the dock supports — a dock capped at 4K/30Hz will visibly stutter a timeline preview compared to one that passes 4K/60Hz or 5K.

Desk light — pro gear

A bias light behind the monitor reduces eye strain during long edit sessions and, on camera, keeps the face from reading darker than the background. The specification to check is CRI — the same one that decides a pocket LED panel — not the lumen count on the box.
