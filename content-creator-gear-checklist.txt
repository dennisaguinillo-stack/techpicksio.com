-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 — MEASURE THESE FOUR THINGS FIRST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
These decide whether the gear you buy physically fits the phone you own.

[ ] Phone width and length in MILLIMETRES, WITH YOUR CASE ON.
        width  ______ mm      length ______ mm
    Cage clamp ranges are stated in mm. A phone that fits bare often
    does not fit cased — this is the most common reason gear goes back.

[ ] Your phone's connector:      [ ] USB-C      [ ] Lightning
    A microphone with the wrong connector is unusable until an adapter
    arrives.

[ ] Does your phone have a MagSafe magnet ring?    [ ] Yes   [ ] No
    iPhones do. Android phones do not. This single fact rules half the
    cage market in or out.

[ ] How long does a typical shoot run for you?  ______ minutes
    This is the number that decides whether a microphone's battery life
    and a light's runtime are actually adequate.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 — THE FOUR ESSENTIALS, IN BUYING ORDER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each one is more useful once the one above it is in place, so working
down the list never leaves anything sitting in a drawer.

[ ] 1. A phone that shoots 4K
       Almost certainly the one in your pocket. Do not upgrade it to
       improve video — the money goes further everywhere else here.

[ ] 2. A wireless lavalier microphone
       The largest single improvement available for the money. Check:
       connector, battery life per charge, transmitter weight.

[ ] 3. A cage or grip that fits your measured width
       Steadies framing and gives the mic and light somewhere to mount.
       Check: clamp range in mm vs. your cased measurement.

[ ] 4. One pocket LED light
       Choose on CRI, not brightness. CRI decides whether skin tones
       look right; most pocket panels are used well below full output.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3 — WORTH ADDING NEXT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] A tripod or stand — move this to #2 if you film yourself alone.
[ ] An ND filter, if you shoot outdoors. It is what lets you hold a
    cinematic shutter angle in daylight.
[ ] Fast external storage, once you record in high-bitrate formats.
    Sustained write speed is the only spec that matters.
[ ] A spare cable and a power bank. Unglamorous, and the two things
    most likely to end a shoot early.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4 — LEAVE THESE FOR LATER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not bad purchases. Badly timed ones.

[ ] A gimbal — revisit when handheld movement genuinely limits a shot
    you want to make. It costs more than the first three purchases
    combined and adds a balancing step before every shoot.
[ ] A drone — revisit when aerial shots are part of your format. Check
    take-off weight against local registration rules first.
[ ] Anamorphic or add-on lenses — revisit once your footage is already
    well lit and well framed.
[ ] A camera body — revisit when a shot you need is impossible, not
    when footage merely feels unpolished.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5 — BEFORE YOUR FIRST SHOOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Phone clamped in the cage FIRST, tightened evenly from both sides
    so it does not sit crooked in the frame.
[ ] Microphone on the TOP cold shoe, not the side. A side mount can end
    up aimed at your own hand.
[ ] LED light set to 30-40% output to start. Phone sensors handle
    highlights poorly and this is almost always enough.
[ ] Cables routed through the cage's channels BEFORE you shoot.
[ ] A 10-second test clip, walking normally, played back on headphones.
    Catches loose screws, cable rattle and clipped audio while they are
    still easy to fix.
